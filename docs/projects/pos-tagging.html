Part-of-speech tagging

Finding the most likely grammatical sequence with a Hidden Markov Model and Viterbi decoding.

Natural language processing · PythonSource code on GitHub →

From words to grammatical roles

A part-of-speech tagger assigns a grammatical label to each word. A Hidden Markov Model combines how likely one tag is to follow another with how likely a word is under a tag. Viterbi finds the most likely complete sequence instead of making independent local choices.

I implemented training and decoding from scratch in Python, with log-space arithmetic to avoid numerical underflow, Laplace smoothing and an unknown-word token. The project achieved approximately 96% per-token accuracy on the WSJ corpus, as reported in my CV.

A small, illustrative HMM for “the birds sing”. Probabilities are chosen for teaching, not taken from the trained model.

See the model and recurrence

score[t,j] = log P(word[t] | j) + maxᵢ(score[t−1,i] + log P(j | i))

Start probabilities [DET, NOUN, VERB] = [0.70, 0.20, 0.10]. Transition rows = [0.05, 0.90, 0.05], [0.10, 0.10, 0.80], [0.60, 0.30, 0.10].

Emission probabilities for [the, birds, sing, other]: DET [0.85, 0.01, 0.01, 0.13]; NOUN [0.01, 0.70, 0.10, 0.19]; VERB [0.01, 0.10, 0.75, 0.14]. Scores use natural logarithms; this fixed-length example has no end-state factor.

Why it works

At each word, keep the best score ending in each possible tag and remember its predecessor. After the last word, choose the best final state and follow those backpointers. With T words and K tags, decoding takes O(TK²) time.

Beyond the toy example

The full implementation explores smoothing strengths of 1.0 and 0.001, lexical ambiguity and out-of-vocabulary inputs. Evaluation includes token- and sentence-level accuracy, confusion matrices and probability visualisations.
