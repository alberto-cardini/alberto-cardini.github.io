Research & BSc thesis

CBCT volumetric denoising

Adapting a 3D residual network to reduce noise while preserving anatomical structure.

Deep learning · Medical imagingResearch project · 2026

The problem

Cone-beam CT reconstructs a three-dimensional volume from X-ray projections. Noise can obscure fine structures; removing it without erasing useful detail is the central challenge.

What I built

My BSc thesis investigated adapting an IDDNet-based 3D residual UNet to a new CBCT domain. I developed data preparation, fine-tuning, inference and evaluation, comparing a standard MSE objective with a custom objective combining L1, structural similarity, PSNR and a penalty on negative outputs.

What changed

Fine-tuning was essential to adapt the pre-trained model to the target distribution. The custom-loss model improved average metric variations relative to RED-CNN in the thesis evaluation:

+0.016additional mean ΔSSIM
4.15%relative gain in mean MSE reduction

Thesis, Table 4.2. The ΔPSNR and ΔSSIM gains correspond to relative improvements of 14.83% and 10.46% in mean improvement over noisy inputs. These are evaluation-set comparisons, not clinical validation.

What I learned

A better average score does not guarantee a better result everywhere. MSE training could introduce structured artifacts; the custom objective could over-smooth fine detail. Looking at failure cases alongside PSNR and SSIM was as important as tracking the averages.

Based on my BSc thesis, CBCT Volumetric Image Denoising based on a Residual UNet, University of Florence, 2025/2026. Supervisor: Andrew D. Bagdanov; co-supervisor: Alice Presenti.
